Critter Camp.

A learning app for children aged 3–6 who can't read yet. One friendly fox, a winding road of small wins, and a voice that carries the whole interface. This is everything the team needs to build v1.

Platform iOS first Stack Flutter + Rive V1 Animals · 10 levels Target TestFlight in ~6 weeks

1 · What we're building

Product definition
AudienceChildren 3–6, pre-literate. They can't read words or numerals — so no text anywhere the child goes, ever.
The buyerThe parent. They install, pay later, and read the progress report behind an adult gate.
V1 scopeOne module — Animals — ten levels, one hero character (Pip the Fox). Alphabets and Numbers come after a real child finishes ten levels unaided.
SessionLevels of 2–4 minutes, chapters of 3–5 levels. One chapter ≈ one 15-minute sitting with a happy ending built in.
Fail stateNone. No lives, timers, scores, or streaks. Wrong answers make the level easier, never harder.
NetworkNone. No backend, no accounts, no analytics SDK. Progress is a local JSON file per child.

2 · Three decisions, already researched

Each of these came out of a deep-research pass. Short version here; ask for the full research doc if you want the citations.

2D, not 3D

Preschool attachment runs on contingency, voice and motion — not rendering. Essentially every category leader (Khan Kids, Duolingo ABC, Pok Pok, Sago Mini) ships stylized 2D. Kids don't prioritize realism; one study found less-realistic characters taught better.

Flutter + Rive

Flutter is our skill and owns the 80% that is UI. Rive is what Duolingo's whole character system runs on: one .riv file, a state machine, named triggers. We remix a CC-BY marketplace rig instead of rigging from scratch. Cost: $0–27.

Road yes, economy no

We keep Candy Crush's winding map — it's genuinely good wayfinding for pre-readers. We strip everything designed to exploit: lives, streaks, near-miss tuning, loss. Those mechanics work on vulnerabilities a 4-year-old can't defend against.

3 · The game loop

The map: one vertical scrolling road, ten nodes in themed chapters (farm → pond → forest). It opens already scrolled to the current node — the only pulsing thing on screen, so the child knows where to tap without reading. Nodes are pictures of the activity, never numbers. Completed nodes show a check and a sticker; future nodes are visible but locked.

A level: enter from the map → Pip speaks the task ("Where is the cow?") → child taps among 3–4 big picture choices → celebration, or a warm hint with one wrong choice removed → back to the map, node done.

The unlock ritual (~1.5s, the only spectacle in the app): Pip walks the road to the next node, it pops from grey to colour, a sticker flies onto the finished one, camera settles on the new current node. Kept short deliberately — research says ornament unrelated to the learning goal breaks learning.

4 · The character

Pip is a Rive rig with one state machine. Aliveness comes from three idle layers that run under everything — see animation-showcase.html for all of this live.

LayerSpec
Breathing3.6s loop, chest scales 1–2%, head follows ~0.2s later (the offset is what reads as alive). First and last frame identical.
BlinkEvery 3–5s on a randomised timer — never synced to the breath loop.
Fidgets3–5 clips (ear flick, head tilt, tail wag), every 30–60s, blended in over 0.2s. A lone idle loop is noticed in ~90 seconds.
TriggerStateNote
tapGigglebounce when the child pokes him

Art style is the cartoon cast (see animation-showcase.html); the naturalistic study (realistic-cast.html) exists for comparison, but evidence favours cartoon for this age.

5 · Voice — the actual interface

  • Every tappable thing names itself when touched. "Cow!", "Stickers!", chapter titles spoken on entry. No text fallback exists.
  • One warm human voice for Pip — recorded slow and a little over-the-top. We record ourselves first; pro VO ($100–500) only if kids don't respond in testing.
  • Per level: one task line, two hints (each more helpful), 4–5 praise variants so praise never repeats back-to-back.
  • Mixing: three channels — VO / SFX / music. Voice always wins: music ducks under it, SFX never cuts it off.
  • Preload every line for the current chapter. A late voice line reads as "broken" to a four-year-old.
  • Files: vo_<level>_<line>.m4a, mapped from the level JSON.

6 · Hard rules

These are researched numbers and commitments, not preferences. They don't get negotiated in code review.

✓Touch targets ≥120pt, ≥100pt apart (2cm — ~7× Apple's 44pt; the HIG minimum is not enough for small fingers)
✓Tap is the only gesture. Any drag also works as tap-then-tap
✓Debounce taps ~600ms; kids multi-tap when they mean once
✓Orientation locked · autosave on every completion
✓Adult gate = arithmetic in words ("what is fourteen plus seven?") before settings, links, purchases, progress report
✓Reduce Motion honoured · feedback never by colour alone
✕No lives, timers, streaks, scores, leaderboards, push notifications
✕No third-party SDKs at all — no Firebase, no analytics, no ads. This is both ethics and Apple Kids Category law (guideline 1.3)
